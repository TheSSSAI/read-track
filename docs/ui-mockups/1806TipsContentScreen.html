How to Build a Consistent Reading Habit in 20 Days

By Sarah Jenkins • Jan 12, 2025

We all have that stack of books on the nightstand waiting to be read. We tell ourselves we'll get to them "when we have time," but that time rarely comes unless we make it. Building a reading habit isn't about finding extra hours in the day; it's about reclaiming small pockets of time and being intentional.

The Power of Habit Stacking

One of the most effective ways to build a new habit is to stack it onto an existing one. This concept, popularized by James Clear in Atomic Habits, leverages your existing neural pathways.

Try this formula: "After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]."

  • "After I pour my morning coffee, I will read two pages."
  • "After I brush my teeth at night, I will read for five minutes."

Start Small (Ridiculously Small)

The biggest mistake new readers make is setting ambitious goals like "read one hour a day." When life gets busy, this goal is the first to go. Instead, set a goal so small you can't say no.

Read one page. Just one. On days you're tired, one page keeps the streak alive. On days you have energy, that one page often turns into ten or twenty.

Environment Design

Design your environment to make reading the path of least resistance. If your book is hidden in a drawer and your phone is on the table, you'll grab the phone. Put a book on your pillow every morning. Keep a book in your bag. Delete social media apps from your home screen.

By reducing the friction to read and increasing the friction to scroll, you naturally shift your behavior.
